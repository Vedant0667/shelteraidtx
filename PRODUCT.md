# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three audiences, all in Dallas-Fort Worth, all arriving on a phone or a laptop with a specific question:

- **Donors and drive hosts.** Parents, teachers, coaches, store managers, and students who have shoes to give or want to run a drive. They want to know in under a minute what is accepted, where to drop off, and that the shoes actually reach people.
- **Shelter staff.** Case managers and directors who need shoes for the people they serve. They want to see who we already work with and how to request a delivery.
- **Supporters and press.** Grant reviewers, sponsors, local media, and other students. They want proof: numbers, partners, faces, the story of how a student-run 501(c)(3) got to 8,000 pairs.

## Product Purpose

Shelter Aid TX collects new and gently used shoes and delivers every pair directly to shelter partners across DFW. The site exists to turn attention into action: a shoe donation, a hosted drive, or a partnership. Success is a visitor who finds the one thing they came to do without hunting, trusts that it works, and does it.

## Brand Personality

**Direct, warm, earned.**

- *Direct*: plain sentences, concrete nouns (pairs, shelters, drop-off points), no nonprofit jargon.
- *Warm*: this is people helping neighbors, photographed in daylight, not a logo grid.
- *Earned*: the numbers and partners are real and recent. Confidence comes from showing them, not from adjectives.

Emotions to evoke: trust first, then pride in the community, then the urge to join in. Never pity, never guilt.

## Anti-references

- Generic nonprofit templates: hero photo, three icon cards, a donate button, a testimonial slider, repeat.
- SaaS landing-page grammar: gradient text, glass cards, tiny uppercase eyebrow above every section, numbered section markers, big-number hero metric tiles with gradient accents.
- Charity guilt aesthetics: desaturated sad photography, urgency banners, countdowns.
- Student-project tells: inconsistent nav across pages, mismatched spacing, placeholder copy, logos in random sizes.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the shoes and the people.** Real photography and real partner logos carry the design. Typography frames them; it does not replace them.
2. **One thing per screen.** Each fold has one idea and one action. Long pages are fine; crowded folds are not.
3. **Proof beats persuasion.** Numbers, partner names, dates, and faces wherever a claim is made. Keep every number current or leave it out.
4. **Same site on every page.** One header, one footer, one type scale, one spacing rhythm, one motion vocabulary. The hero sets the tone; every other page inherits it.
5. **Fast and readable on a phone.** Body text passes 4.5:1 contrast, line length stays under 70 characters, and nothing depends on hover.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, focus visibility, keyboard reachability for the menu, tabs, accordion, and forms.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: counters and reveals resolve instantly, marquees stop.
- Alt text describes the photo's content, not "image".
- Forms label every field, show inline errors in words, and never rely on color alone.
- Shoe sizes and drop-off details must be readable without zooming on a 375px screen.
